Technical Case Studies

Archival Context: Independent community archives often handle rapid, high-volume ingestions of fragile media formats with lean staffing models. The case studies below demonstrate my practical competency in building programmatic preservation guardrails. Each module acts as a reproducible template for stabilizing incoming collections, protecting data integrity, and expanding community access pathways.

1. Audiovisual Cataloging using PBCore

Objective: Map moving image parameters using a structured schema paired with radical community-led vocabularies to maximize marginalized historical discovery.

Context & Why It Matters: Traditional metadata structures often fail to capture critical technical specifications of analog tapes, leading to poor preservation. By pairing the technical taxonomy of PBCore with the progressive identity terminology of the Homosaurus, I built a record that ensures historical media remains both functionally sustainable and accurately discoverable for researchers.

Syntax Overview:

<pbcoreDescriptionDocument xmlns="http://pbcore.org">
  <pbcoreIdentifier source="Local ID">QA-1992-OH-042</pbcoreIdentifier>
  <pbcoreTitle titleType="Formal">Interview with June Mazer and Activists</pbcoreTitle>
  <pbcoreSubject source="Homosaurus" ref="https://homosaurus.org">Lesbian activism</pbcoreSubject>
  <pbcoreInstantiation>
    <instantiationDigital>video/mp4</instantiationDigital>
  </pbcoreInstantiation>
</pbcoreDescriptionDocument>

2. Digital Preservation & Ingest using BagIt

Objective: Ensure baseline file integrity and safe transfers for incoming digitized community collections, protecting files against bit rot.

Context & Why It Matters: Digital materials are profoundly fragile; files can silently corrupt over time simply by being moved between drives. This workflow utilizes the Library of Congress BagIt specification (RFC 8493) to wrap archival digital objects from the Lydia R. Otero Archive at Los Angeles Public Library Special Collections into a secure preservation package, embedding cryptographic fingerprints (SHA-256) to verify data fixity upon ingest.

Directory Architecture:

lydia_otero_digital_preservation_demo/
├── 01_source_metadata/      # Descriptive metadata & inventory logs
├── 03_bag/                  # Structured preservation package
│   ├── bagit.txt            # Declares RFC 8493 formatting
│   ├── manifest-sha256.txt  # SHA-256 fixity check fingerprints
│   └── data/                # Payload (Digitized archival ephemera)
├── 04_documentation/        # Provenance records & validation logs
└── 05_screenshots/          # Controlled fixity check test results

3. Web Archiving & Capturing Grassroots Ephemera

Objective: Capture, preserve, and render high-fidelity, interactive snapshots of ephemeral community websites and online mutual aid directories to prevent link rot.

Context & Why It Matters: Modern community history unfolds rapidly on platforms that vanish without notice when hosting fees lapse or group dynamics shift. This study shows how to crawl reactive, JavaScript-heavy community bulletin spaces to lock them into standardized, archival WARC containers—safeguarding the authentic interactive experience of internet history.

Integration Syntax:

<script src="https://unpkg.com"></script>
<replay-web-page 
  src="assets/warc/local-lgbtq-mutual-aid-2026.warc" 
  url="https://example-mutual-aid-group.org">
</replay-web-page>
